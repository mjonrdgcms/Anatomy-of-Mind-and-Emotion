# The Approach Wheel: How the Two Diagrams Work

A reading of the two images the author supplied (the nested "Approaches" heptagon with the "Types of Data" legend, and the large tool-and-weapon wheel), checked against the 2025 Text Analysis file. The author's statement of confidence: the theory is very accurate at the level of the dichotomies and the seven approaches; the individual tools and weapons are tentative; the cognitive-function labels in the text file are a loose explanatory device only.

## 1. The heptagon

Seven approaches are the seven sides of a heptagon with a vertex at the top. Clockwise from the top:

| Position | Approach | Number | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper right | Expansion | 3 | green |
| Right | Transformation | 7 | purple |
| Lower right | Unification | 4 | gold |
| Bottom | Implementation | 5 | red |
| Lower left | Initiation | 1 | orange |
| Left | Deconstruction | 2 | blue |
| Upper left | Preservation | 6 | teal |

Neighbours matter: the two approaches that share a vertex with a given approach are its adjacent ones, and the vertex directly opposite an approach lies between two other approaches. That is why everything on the wheel has two opposites.

## 2. The seven dichotomies

Each ring of the nested diagram is one dichotomy. Each dichotomy cuts the heptagon into two poles with a single straight line that runs from one vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. So each pole holds three whole approaches and half of a fourth, and exactly one approach is cut in half by each dichotomy. The measured boundaries:

| Dichotomy (dark pole / light pole) | Line runs through | Approach it cuts in half | Dark pole holds | Light pole holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct / Indirect | midpoint of Preservation, vertex Implementation-Unification | Preservation | Deconstruction, Initiation, Implementation, ½ Preservation | Unification, Transformation, Expansion, ½ Preservation |
| Specific / General | midpoint of Expansion, vertex Initiation-Implementation | Expansion | Preservation, Deconstruction, Initiation, ½ Expansion | Implementation, Unification, Transformation, ½ Expansion |
| Future / Present | midpoint of Unification, vertex Preservation-Deconstruction | Unification | Preservation, Expansion, Transformation, ½ Unification | Deconstruction, Initiation, Implementation, ½ Unification |
| Interactive / Speculative | midpoint of Initiation, vertex Transformation-Expansion | Initiation | Implementation, Unification, Transformation, ½ Initiation | Expansion, Preservation, Deconstruction, ½ Initiation |
| Simple (linear) / Complex (nonlinear) | midpoint of Deconstruction, vertex Unification-Transformation | Deconstruction | Initiation, Implementation, Unification, ½ Deconstruction | Transformation, Expansion, Preservation, ½ Deconstruction |
| Process (bottom up) / Result (top down) | midpoint of Transformation, vertex Deconstruction-Initiation | Transformation | Expansion, Preservation, Deconstruction, ½ Transformation | Initiation, Implementation, Unification, ½ Transformation |
| Flexible (impartial) / Rigid (partial) | midpoint of Implementation, vertex at top | Implementation | Unification, Transformation, Expansion, ½ Implementation | Preservation, Deconstruction, Initiation, ½ Implementation |

Two rules fall out of the picture:

- **The approach a dichotomy cuts in half is the one that bridges it.** This matches the bridges in *Beyond Flat Land*: Initiation bridges interactive and speculative, Deconstruction simple and complex, Expansion specific and general, Unification future and present, Implementation flexible and rigid, Preservation direct and indirect, Transformation process and result.
- **Each ring is coloured by the approach sitting whole in the middle of its dark pole.** Direct is orange (Initiation in the middle), Specific blue (Deconstruction), Future green (Expansion), Interactive gold (Unification), Simple red (Implementation), Process teal (Preservation), Flexible purple (Transformation). The colour names the approach that most purely embodies that pole; the bridging approach is a different one, at the edge of the pole.

## 3. Each approach is defined by six poles and one bridge

Because every approach lies wholly inside one pole of six dichotomies and is cut by the seventh, each approach is a fixed combination:

| Approach | Wholly inside | Cut by (its bridge) |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | Direct, Specific, Present, Simple, Result, Rigid | Interactive / Speculative |
| Deconstruction | Direct, Specific, Present, Speculative, Process, Rigid | Simple / Complex |
| Expansion | Indirect, Future, Speculative, Complex, Process, Flexible | Specific / General |
| Unification | Indirect, General, Interactive, Simple, Result, Flexible | Future / Present |
| Implementation | Direct, General, Present, Interactive, Simple, Result | Flexible / Rigid |
| Preservation | Specific, Future, Speculative, Complex, Process, Rigid | Direct / Indirect |
| Transformation | Indirect, General, Future, Interactive, Complex, Flexible | Process / Result |

This is what the author means by "how each tool or weapon within it is defined is by the dichotomies it is part of." Every one of the twelve "Only part of X that is Y" sentences in the text file describes one of the fourteen half-sides, and every one of them checks out against the table above. Two examples:

- "Only part of the Future that is simple is also general, interactive, result, flexible. Hope." Future ∩ Simple is the half of Unification nearest Transformation. Unification is wholly general, interactive, simple, result, flexible, and Hope is one of its tools on that half.
- "Only part of the present that is complex is specific, speculative, process, rigid." Present ∩ Complex is the half of Deconstruction nearest Preservation. Deconstruction is wholly specific, speculative, process, rigid.

## 4. The two halves of each approach are its asset half and its risk half

On the big wheel every side carries a row of eight symbols, and on every side, read clockwise, the row is the same:

```
┘  ■  ◗  ●  |  ○  ◐  □  ┌
```

The first four are filled shapes, the last four are hollow. The legend says filled is **asset** and hollow is **risk**. So the half of each side that comes first going clockwise is the asset half, and the half that follows is the risk half, and the bridge dichotomy is exactly the line that separates them. That gives each approach's two halves a pole each:

| Approach | Asset half (extraverted tools) sits in | Risk half (introverted tools) sits in |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | Interactive | Speculative |
| Deconstruction | Simple | Complex |
| Expansion | Specific | General |
| Unification | Future | Present |
| Implementation | Flexible | Rigid |
| Preservation | Direct | Indirect |
| Transformation | Process | Result |

The opening lines of the text file describe each approach as a movement from its asset pole to its risk pole: Initiation goes from courage "with the interactive" to recognition "of results" (speculative); Deconstruction from data collection about the simple to defining variables in relation to the complex; Expansion from specific iterations to a general theory; Implementation from flexible planning to commitment; Preservation from direct incoming radar to indirect pre-emptive response; Transformation from reflecting on results to contemplating ideals.

## 5. The eight types of data

The legend is a 2 × 2 × 2 cube:

| | Concrete (square family) | Abstract (round family) |
|---|---|---|
| **Asset, raw** | ■ | ● |
| **Asset, interpreted** | ┘ (filled corner) | ◗ (filled crescent) |
| **Risk, interpreted** | ┌ (hollow corner) | ◐ (hollow crescent) |
| **Risk, raw** | □ | ○ |

Raw data is the whole shape; interpreted data is the shape with a piece taken out of it. Concrete is angular, abstract is round.

The cognitive-function labels in the text file map onto this cube one to one, which is the only sense in which they fit:

- Extraverted = asset-appreciating (filled). Introverted = risk-appreciating (hollow). Nothing to do with people or social situations.
- Thinking and Sensing = concrete. Feeling and Intuition = abstract.
- Thinking and Feeling = interpreted. Sensing and Intuition = raw.

So Te = ┘, Se = ■, Fe = ◗, Ne = ●, Ni = ○, Fi = ◐, Si = □, Ti = ┌, which is the symbol row in order. (Implementation's label row on the wheel is in exactly this order. On some other sides the two labels within a pair are drawn in the other order; since the labels are tentative and the pairs are bricked in a staggered bond, I have taken the symbol row as the definition and the file's function tags as the tie-breaker. This is one of the points to confirm below.)

One correspondence with the dream grammar is worth noting without asserting it: the concrete family is square and the abstract family is round, which is the mind's shape and the heart's shape in *Four Rivers*. The wheel's raw/interpreted split may be the same thing as the dream grammar's raw experience (kitchen) versus processed memory (library).

## 6. The 56 tools and 56 weapons

Each approach has four pair-tools (one per quadrant of the cube: concrete risk, concrete asset, abstract risk, abstract asset), each pair splitting into an interpreted tool and a raw tool. Each tool has a weapon, its overuse. The full table is in `grammar/approaches.json`; the summary:

| Approach | Concrete risk (┌ □) | Concrete asset (┘ ■) | Abstract risk (◐ ○) | Abstract asset (◗ ●) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Initiation** | Functionality: Emulation, Recognition. Weapon Intimidation: Unoriginality, Jealousy | Confidence: Decisiveness, Options. Competitive: Impatience, Indecision | Cleverness: Strategy, Inference. Cheating: Indiscriminate, Scapegoating | Enterprise: Autonomy, Anticipation. Exploitation: Audacity, Conjecture |
| **Deconstruction** | Empiricism: Categorization, Corroboration. Intolerance: Hypercritical, Skeptical | Objectivity: Accuracy, Concentration. Self-fulfilling: Selectivity, Rumination | Causation: Insight, Antithesis. Instigation: Superstition, Rebelliousness | Subjectivity: Context, Immersion. Misuse: Stereotype, Indulgence |
| **Expansion** | Humility: Learning, Patience. Laziness: Skimming, Procrastination | Innovation: Pioneering, Openness. Entertainment: Ostentatious, Paradox | Holism: Implications, Consideration. Eccentric: Desperate, Childishness | Imagination: Vision, Creativity. Helplessness: Wishfulness, Distractibility |
| **Unification** | Continuity: Maintenance, Follow-up. Tyranny: Invasiveness, Nosey | Receptivity: Amenability, Responsiveness. Erraticness: Permissiveness, Fakeness | Synergy: Tact, Compatibility. Enmeshment: Self-effacing, Hypersensitivity | Hope: Opportunity, Optimism. Passive-aggressive: Dramatic, Prattling |
| **Implementation** | Completeness: Commitment, Honesty. Defensive: Hiding, Scrupulousness | Production: Structure, Diligence. Coarseness: Overreach, Stubbornness | Momentum: Stability, Resilience. Restlessness: Unadventurousness, Toiling | Belief: Principles, Enthusiasm. Mania: Ephemeral, Relentlessness |
| **Preservation** | Preparation: Utility, Organization. Preemptive: Cowardice, Perfectionism | Budgeting: Prioritization, Fortification. Corruption: Favoritism, Redundancy | Calculation: Acumen, Caution. Pessimism: Avoidant, Hypochondria | Salvaging: Repurposing, Acceptance. Greed: Hoarding, Liquidating |
| **Transformation** | Attitude: Transcendence, Reframing. Apathy: Rationalization, Flippancy | Fulfillment: Presence, Gratitude. Tantrum: Entitlement, Naive | Idealism: Ethics, Optimization. Extreme: Dogmatic, Vulgar | Character: Actualization, Aspiration. Obsession: Nicheness, Envy |

In each cell the first name of a pair is the interpreted tool (T or F in the file) and the second is the raw tool (S or N).

So a single tool is fully located by seven poles plus a data type. Example: **Hope** is Unification (indirect, general, interactive, simple, result, flexible), on the asset half (future), abstract, and its two tools are Opportunity (interpreted, ◗) and Optimism (raw, ●). Its weapon is Passive-aggression, split into Dramatic and Prattling.

The axes named for the emotions in `docs/seven-emotions.md` turn out to be tool names on the risk half of each approach: functionality (Initiation, concrete risk), reproducibility (Deconstruction's Empiricism word list), perspective (Expansion's Holism word list), harmony (Unification's Synergy), stability (Implementation's Momentum), certainty (Preservation's Calculation and Preparation), excellence (Transformation's Idealism and Optimization). The emotion, in other words, is felt on the risk side, which is where the introverted, hollow, exposure-facing tools sit.

## 7. Opposites

The point directly across the wheel from a side is a vertex, which belongs to two sides. So every approach has two opposites, and so does every tool and weapon within it:

| Approach | Its two opposites |
|---|---|
| Initiation | Expansion and Transformation |
| Deconstruction | Transformation and Unification |
| Expansion | Implementation and Initiation |
| Unification | Deconstruction and Preservation |
| Implementation | Preservation and Expansion |
| Preservation | Unification and Implementation |
| Transformation | Initiation and Deconstruction |

The finer geometry says which half faces which: an asset half always faces a risk half. Initiation's asset half faces Expansion's risk half, and Initiation's risk half faces Transformation's asset half; Implementation's asset half faces Preservation's risk half, and its risk half faces Expansion's asset half; and so on around the wheel. Every asset-risk pairing across the wheel disagrees on all seven dichotomies except one: Initiation's asset half (direct, specific, present, simple, result, rigid, interactive) faces Expansion's risk half (indirect, general, future, complex, process, flexible, general). They share nothing. That is what makes them opposites and not merely different.

**The early survey.** Ask which three weapons a person dislikes most. The opposite tools and weapons, across the wheel, are where that person spends their time. This was very predictive. The mechanism in the theory's own terms: a strong dislike of a weapon is a taboo on the whole approach that weapon belongs to, and a taboo on one approach means the two approaches across from it are carrying the load, in both their tool and weapon forms.

## 8. The text-analysis dictionaries

The 2025 file attaches a word list to every tool and weapon (for most of them, several hundred inflected forms). That is a scoring instrument: count the words in a text that belong to each tool and weapon, and the profile tells you which approaches are running and whether they are running as tools or as weapons. Its use for the dream interpreter is direct. A dream transcript and the dreamer's waking account can both be scored, and the interpreter can compare which approaches the heart is running in the dream against which ones the palette runs by day. The dictionaries are unfinished (the author's note), so the interpreter should treat scores as a prompt for a question, not as a finding.

## 9. What I would like the author to confirm

1. **Raw versus interpreted within a pair.** I have read the symbol row as ┘ ■ ◗ ● ○ ◐ □ ┌ meaning Te Se Fe Ne Ni Fi Si Ti, so T and F are the interpreted tools (corner, crescent) and S and N the raw ones (square, circle). Implementation's labels on the wheel are in exactly that order. Deconstruction, Unification and some others have the two labels of a pair drawn the other way round. Which is right: the symbol row, or the label order on each side?
2. **Opposites at the tool level.** Across the wheel, is the opposite of a given tool the tool with the same data-type symbol in each of the two facing approaches, or is it the mirrored data type (asset faces risk, as the geometry suggests)? The survey method would work either way, but the interpreter needs one rule.
3. **The dark pole.** Each dichotomy's dark pole is the one that holds the asset half of the bridging approach, and it is coloured by the approach in its middle. Is the dark pole meant as the "asset" or "extraverted" side of the dichotomy in general, or is the colouring only a visual aid?
4. **Square and round.** Is the concrete/abstract split of the data cube the same distinction as the mind/heart split of the dream grammar (square versus round, measurable versus immeasurable)? If so, the Types of Data legend is a bridge between the two books that the interpreter can use directly.

# The Seven Dichotomy Cards

Companion to `docs/cards.md`. One card per dichotomy, playing-card size, front and back. Each dichotomy is defined by its **dominant approach** (the one sitting whole in the middle of its dark pole, which gives the pole its colour), the **mirror** (the approach in the middle of the light pole, always the dominant one's opposite across the wheel), and the two dichotomies that **overlap it most** (the two whose cut lines sit one step either side on the heptagon; they agree on two and a half sides and differ on one side at each end). Everything here was computed from the wheel geometry in `docs/approach-wheel.md`, not read by eye.

Three facts that hold for all seven and belong on the key card:

- The dominant approach and its mirror are always opposites across the wheel. A dichotomy is the border between two opposite territories.
- Each pole holds three whole approaches and half of a fourth. The half is the approach that bridges the dichotomy: its asset tools sit on the dark pole, its risk tools on the light pole.
- Two neighbouring dichotomies share two and a half sides. What they do not share is always one whole approach on one side and two half-approaches on the other, so the difference between any two dichotomies can be named by tools.

## Layout shared by all seven

**Front**
- Band in the dominant approach's colour: the dichotomy as *Dark pole / Light pole*.
- Two columns under the band. Left, the dark pole: its name, its one-line meaning, **dominant** approach, and the three and a half approaches it holds. Right, the light pole: the same, with **mirror** instead of dominant.
- Across the middle: *Bridged by* the approach the line cuts, with "asset tools left, risk tools right".
- The drawing below, split down the middle like the card: the dark pole's image on the left, the light pole's on the right.
- Bottom: a tiny heptagon with the cut line drawn and the dark pole shaded.

**Back**
- Band: *Most like:* the two neighbouring dichotomies.
- For each neighbour, three short rows: **Agree on** (the shared sides), **This alone** (what is on this dark pole and not the neighbour's), **That alone** (what is on the neighbour's and not this). Each "alone" row names the approach or the half-approach and its tools, so the difference is concrete.
- Bottom line in italics: the question that tells the two apart.

## Direct / Indirect (orange)

**Front**
- **Direct**: protect it, or meet it, directly; the incoming radar. Dominant: **Initiation**. Holds Deconstruction, Initiation, Implementation, and the asset half of Preservation.
- **Indirect**: buffers, records, redundancies and habits that protect it later; the pre-emptive response. Mirror: **Transformation**. Holds Unification, Transformation, Expansion, and the risk half of Preservation.
- Bridged by **Preservation**: Budgeting and Salvaging on the Direct side; Preparation and Calculation on the Indirect side.

**Drawing.** Left: a radar dish turned toward something approaching, a single bright blip on its screen. Right: a ledger open on a shelf, dated today, and through a window beside it a calendar page for a month ahead. The dish faces the thing; the ledger faces the future.

**Back**
- Most like **Specific** and **Simple**.
- *Specific.* Agree on: Deconstruction, Initiation, and Preservation's asset half. Direct alone: **Implementation** whole (Production, Belief, Completeness, Momentum): committing to one course for everyone, which is direct but general. Specific alone: Expansion's asset half (Innovation, Imagination) and Preservation's risk half (Preparation, Calculation): iterating and preparing for a particular case, which is specific but indirect.
- *Simple.* Agree on: Implementation, Initiation, and Deconstruction's asset half. Direct alone: Deconstruction's risk half (Empiricism, Causation) and Preservation's asset half (Budgeting, Salvaging): meeting the thing head-on in its tangle. Simple alone: **Unification** whole (Receptivity, Hope, Continuity, Synergy): the plain account that goes through other people.
- *Is this handled now, in front of me, or set up in advance to handle itself?*

**Drawing.** Left: a road leaving a doorway, running to a horizon with a faint sunrise. Right: the same doorway seen from inside, a table set for the people who are there, food on it, steam rising. One doorway, drawn once on the centre line, with the road on one side and the table on the other.

**Back**
- Most like **Process** and **Flexible**.
- *Process.* Agree on: Expansion, Preservation, and Transformation's asset half. Future alone: Transformation's risk half (Attitude, Idealism) and Unification's asset half (Receptivity, Hope): the ideal and the hope that pull forward. - *Flexible.* Agree on: Expansion, Transformation, and Unification's asset half. Flexible alone: Implementation's asset half (Production, Belief) and Unification's risk half (Continuity, Synergy): keeping the present working while options stay open.
- *Is this about where we are going, or whether what we have fits together now?*

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct / Indirect | Initiation | Transformation | Preservation | Specific, Simple |
| Specific / General | Deconstruction | Unification | Expansion | Direct, Process |
| Future / Present | Expansion | Initiation | Unification | Process, Flexible |
| Interactive / Speculative | Unification | Preservation | Initiation | Simple, Flexible |
| Simple / Complex | Implementation | Expansion | Deconstruction | Direct, Interactive |
| Process / Result | Preservation | Implementation | Transformation | Specific, Future |
| Flexible / Rigid | Transformation | Deconstruction | Implementation | Future, Interactive |

Each approach dominates exactly one dark pole and mirrors exactly one light pole, so the seven approaches and the fourteen poles are one system, not two.

# How a Dream Is Read With This Grammar

This is the procedure an interpreter (human or model) follows. It is derived from the worked readings in the books (the kitchen, the sea of glass, the money changers, the she-bear, the coin in the fish, the bathroom, the teeth) and from the guards in *Beyond Flat Land* chapter 25.

## Posture

- The interpreter holds the grammar. The dreamer holds the facts. Neither can do the other's job.
- The dream is the heart's picture of the situation the dreamer is already in. It is not a message from elsewhere, not an instruction, not a prediction, and never evidence about another person.
- The reading is finished when it names what got sorted into the wrong box and hands back one checkable thing. It is not finished when it sounds good.

## Step 0. Intake

Collect, in the dreamer's own words:

1. The dream, as fully as it can be recalled, including what the dreamer knew inside the dream (e.g. "I knew the house was gone").
2. Whether it is one of a series (last night, the night before) or recurring.
3. The feeling on waking.
4. Optional: what has been going on in waking life. If not offered, ask after the first pass, not before, so the reading is not steered.

## Step 1. Which kind of dream

Default to the third kind, the dream from the self. Only treat a dream as being about outside events if the dreamer insists and the elements are plainly literal people and events; even then, apply the guard: a dream is a hypothesis about the dreamer's model of the world, never confirmation of the world.

## Step 2. Inventory the elements and assign countries

List every element: setting, people, animals, objects, substances, vehicles, weather, actions.

For each, count the markers from each country (low/high, dark/light, round/square, water/hard, mammal/reptile, gold/silver, feminine/masculine, left/right). Note where **two or more markers from one country stack in a single image**; that is the tell that the dream register is at full strength there.

Note compounds: a round silver thing, a river on a mountain, a tree in a kitchen. These are sentences, not contradictions.

## Step 3. Read the self

- **House**: which rooms, which doors and windows, what is sealed, what will not shut, whether there is a roof or the roof is opened. Kitchen and bathroom get read as preparation and release. Teeth as assumptions. Table as attention.
- **Tree / staff / cut wood**: the will, and what state it is in (rooted, cut down, budded, laid down).
- **Vessel / cup / bowl**: the heart and what it is holding, whether it is open at the top.
- **Stone, sand, desert, library, boulder, glass**: the state of memory and testimony.

## Step 4. Read the people

For every person in the dream, **ask the dreamer for three words that describe that person.** Do not guess. Then read the words as organs of the dreamer's own psyche wearing that face. Check the compression question: does the figure have the person's real range, or has the person been reduced to one function, and what did that reduction let the dream do?

Babies and children: a new part of the dreamer, born where heart and mind came together, more integrated with logic and love than what came before. Name what was recently born, and read the dream as being about how it is being fed.

## Step 5. Read the animals

For every animal, **ask who the animal is** (which relationship). Then read the species as the facet through which the dreamer interfaces with that person (dog: loyalty; horse: motivation; cat: independence; cow: giving; sheep: comfort; bear: the mother; wolf: autonomy; lion: courage). The reading runs both ways: is that what this person should be to the dreamer?

Serpents are not relationships. They are attention. Read where attention is (on the ground, lifted, sunk), what it has swallowed, and whether it has bitten.

Insects are the heart's guards: mindless and protective, good or bad. Ask what they are swarming toward, and whether the thing being protected is a wound or a husk.

## Step 6. Read the vehicles

Capacity and rails. Read what the dream says about how the dreamer is moving through society: on whose route, in a vehicle sized for whom, whether the built thing has foundered on the water and a living thing has had to do the carrying. Flying: built or given.

## Step 7. Read the water

Where is the water, and what is it doing: still (mirror), moving (going somewhere), flooding (banks failed), marsh (no banks), ice (fear), poured out, opened from a rock or a side. Which of the four rivers is running, and in which position are the people standing.

## Step 8. The four horns

For the central relationship or thing in the dream, ask whether it is tied at one horn or four: can it stand face to face, side by side, behind, and back to back? A thing tied to one post circles. Identify which leg of the table is overextended and what slid off.

## Step 9. Name the sorting error

This is the headline. Say plainly which of these the dream is showing:

- A **heart thing handled with the mind's instruments**: something immeasurable being weighed, counted, gridded, catalogued, put in rows, defended as a record, carved into a fixed shape (proxy capture; the idol; the money changers' tables; the stone heart).
- A **mind thing dropped into the heart's water**: something that needed banks, a definition, a test, a decision, left to float; the ground not holding, water where the floor should be, a house adrift (meaning as amnesty; the flood; the marsh).
- Or the ordinary daily case: the day's mixed seed, being sorted, with one item flagged.

If the second book's layer is in use, add: which emotion-verb the dream is delivering (take, hold, let go, isolate, expand, incorporate, receive), and whether the situation is joy without peace or peace without joy.

## Step 10. Check the reading against the guards

Before offering it, confirm the reading:

- accounts for several important features, not one convenient symbol;
- connects to something specific in waking life (or asks for it);
- does not require ignoring the elements that push the other way (say which they are);
- generates a question, an observation, or a lamp-scale action that can be checked outside the dream;
- makes no claim about what any other person thinks, did, or intends;
- does not increase certainty faster than it increases contact with reality.

Resonance is weak evidence. If the reading "lands" but fails the guards, it is set down.

## Step 11. Series and recurrence

If the dream is one of a series: read the first as context, the second as emphasis, the third as depth, and look for the narrowing. If a dream is recurring, the same sentence is being established; the question is what in waking life has not moved.

## Step 12. Output shape

1. **The sentence.** One or two lines: what the dream is saying, in the grammar.
2. **The elements.** Each symbol, its country, its reading, and how it joins the others.
3. **What got sorted wrong.** The misfiled thing, named.
4. **The contrary evidence.** What in the dream does not fit this reading.
5. **One thing to check.** A question for waking life, or one small action, at lamp-scale.
6. **What the interpreter needs from the dreamer** (three words, who the animal is, waking context), if the reading is provisional.

Keep readings short. The dream is not solved; it is carried.
